## Day 1 — Drill vs. Impact Driver

**Facebook / Instagram copy**

A drill and an impact driver are not the same tool. A drill gives you clutch control and handles general drilling. An impact driver delivers more torque for long screws, deck work, framing and repeated fastening. If you are buying only one tool for household work, start with the drill. If you already own a drill and drive a lot of screws, the impact driver is the upgrade you will notice immediately.

## Day 3 — Card Storage

**Facebook / Instagram copy**

The easiest way to damage a valuable card is during sorting and handling. Use three storage levels: boxes for bulk cards, sleeves plus top loaders for better cards, and a properly sized rigid holder plus team bag for higher-value cards. Never force a thick card into a standard holder.

## Day 6 — Buying Used Tools

**Facebook / Instagram copy**

Used tools can be a strong value if you inspect them correctly. Check the cord or battery terminals, trigger response, chuck or arbor play, unusual bearing noise, burning smell and signs of water damage. For battery tools, confirm the battery and charger work before treating the price as a deal.

Shop carefully and compare against new-tool pricing before buying.

**YouTube Short script**

Hook: “Buying a used power tool? Check this before paying.”  
Body: “Test the trigger, listen for bearing noise, check the chuck or arbor for play, inspect the terminals, and smell for overheated windings. For cordless tools, make sure the battery and charger are actually included and working.”  
CTA: “A low price is not a deal if the tool is already failing.”
