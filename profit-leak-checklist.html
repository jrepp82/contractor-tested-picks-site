Contractor Profit Leak Checklist

Twelve places money commonly disappears between the estimate, the jobsite and the final payment.

How to use this

Run one active or recently completed job through every item below. Mark anything you cannot answer with a real number. Those are the first places to tighten up.

1. Material cost is current

Did the estimate use current supplier pricing, quantities, waste and material sales tax instead of an old allowance?

Check: supplier total vs. estimated material total.

2. Labor hours are realistic

Did you price the hours the work will actually take—including setup, handling, cleanup and unavoidable non-install time?

Check: estimated hours vs. actual hours.

3. Your labor rate covers the business

A wage is not a billable rate. The selling rate has to support payroll burden, downtime, vehicles, tools, insurance and operating overhead.

Check: billable rate vs. true hourly business cost.

4. Overhead is not forgotten—or counted twice

Know where overhead enters the price. Missing it loses money; applying it again after it is already embedded inflates the quote and can kill the sale.

Check: one documented overhead treatment.

5. Profit is intentional

If the job only makes money when everything goes perfectly, the price is too fragile.

Check: selling price minus all expected job and business costs.

6. Change orders happen before extra work

Added scope should have a written description, price and approval before the extra labor or material is consumed whenever practical.

Check: approved changes vs. unpaid extras.

7. Material runs and small consumables are captured

Fasteners, blades, caulk, fuel, pickup trips and small purchases become real money across multiple jobs.

Check: actual receipts and trips against the estimate.

8. Equipment, disposal and access are priced

Dumpsters, lifts, scaffolding, rentals, delivery, hauling and difficult access do not become free just because they are not part of the finished product.

Check: every direct job cost has a home in the price.

9. Deposit and progress payments protect cash flow

Do not finance the customer's project with your own cash if the contract and local rules allow a better payment structure.

Check: cash collected before major cash outlays.

10. Open estimates are followed up

An estimate that never gets a follow-up is paid acquisition and estimating time with no second attempt to close the work.

Check: every open estimate has a next-contact date.

11. Receivables have a next action

Finished work does not become income until the money is collected.

Check: every unpaid invoice has an amount, due date and follow-up action.

12. Every completed job gets a post-job review

The fastest way to improve future pricing is to compare estimated cost and hours with what actually happened.

Check: estimated cost, actual cost, gross profit and gross margin.
